# AEGIS 4.0 — Synthetic Resilience Benchmark v0.2

**DATA STATUS: 100% SYNTHETIC.** Neither transaction counts nor risk scores nor modeled cost impacts are actual corporate outcomes. This dataset was created for controlled algorithm development, not to establish real-world ROI. Do not describe it as primary field research.

## Reason for v0.2
Version 0.1 sampled procurement purchase orders independently of customer demand and inventory. It therefore could not support credible end-to-end replenishment evaluation. **v0.2 replaces the procurement generator with a chronological, demand-responsive policy.** The two versions should not be mixed; results using v0.1 will not be directly comparable to results on v0.2.

## Dataset lineage
The ten synthetic master/event/demand tables in `seed_data/` are archived from AEGIS v0.1 and remain synthetic. `generate_v02.py` copies those into `data/`, then derives `purchase_orders.csv`, `component_inventory_monthly.csv`, and `data_dictionary.csv`. All data is generated reproducibly; no real company datasets are included.

## Core data process
1. Daily customer demand originates in the synthetic `customer_demand_daily.csv`.
2. Each daily SKU demand is exploded into its component requirements using the `bill_of_materials.csv` and warehouse-to-primary-plant mapping.
3. Every seven days, planners forecast component requirements from a **trailing 28-day window** of previously realized demand and a disclosed 14-day smoothing prior based on a hypothetical launch plan (10 SKU units/day/warehouse).
4. Planners select suppliers from qualified alternatives with a documented weekly qualification-rotation rule. Purchases are triggered when on-hand plus open pipeline is below target inventory coverage. Quantities comply with supplier-option MOQs and synthetic monthly booking capacities. This is a deliberately simple **baseline purchasing policy**, not an optimized AEGIS recovery strategy.
5. Supplier shipment delays arise from a synthetic latent reliability process, order-time congestion, urgent orders, regionally published alerts, and **future realized** random shocks. Realized shock severity is used only by the synthetic outcome generator; it is NEVER an order-time model feature.
6. Actual receipts update stock on their arrival days. Component consumption is simulated daily, and monthly ledgers aggregate receipts, requested/consumed/unmet units, and opening/closing stock. These are component shortages, not proven finished-goods stockouts.

## Key simulation assumptions and limitations
- Orders happen **at the start of each seventh day**; receipt and customer-demand realizations occur afterward, preventing same-day leakage.
- The generated PO fields `forecast_daily_requirements_at_order`, `inventory_on_hand_at_order`, `inventory_on_order_at_order`, `reorder_target_units_at_order`, and `inventory_position_at_order` are known when that PO was placed, by construction.
- Supplier history reflects only orders received **strictly before** each PO creation date; the late rate is smoothed with a (2 successes, 8 failures) pseudo-count of 10, so no-history late rate is 20%.
- Region alerts use `alert_published_date` and a fixed published validity window of 10 days (an artificial simulation rule). Unknown actual event end dates or realized severity are **not** exposed to the order-time prediction system.
- A simulated operation can show synthetic supplier losses and simulated component shortages, not independently verified company profitability, causal lift, observed disruption recovery or real commercial suitability.
- No full production schedule, finished-goods delivery ledger, exchange-rate realism, capacity calendar (beyond monthly supplier option booking limits), procurement cancellations, or confirmed causal treatment effects are modeled. These remain extensions for later phases.
- 60 master suppliers exist, 58 are in the synthetic approved component options, and 57 receive actual purchase orders in this particular baseline policy run. No assumption that all listed suppliers are transacted.
- Correlation between ordered quantities and requirements is a diagnostic, **not** proof of causal validity or predictive performance. An offline counterfactual optimization needs a separate, independently checked policy-evaluation protocol.
- Performance on data generated by this simulator reflects its assumptions; external validation needs real public/corporate data, which is not yet part of this version.

## Key tables
All inside `data/`:
- `purchase_orders.csv`: 12,544 orders with order-time planning features, realized delivery outcomes and target (`late_receipt_flag`).
- `customer_demand_daily.csv`: 65,664 synthetic daily SKU/warehouse demand records.
- `component_inventory_monthly.csv`: 3,600 reconciled monthly plant-component stock balances.
- Master/link tables: `suppliers.csv`, `components.csv`, `plants.csv`, `warehouses.csv`, `finished_products.csv`, `bill_of_materials.csv`, `supplier_component_options.csv`, `transport_lanes.csv`.
- Event table: `disruption_events.csv`.
- `data_dictionary.csv`: source/availability metadata.

## Predictor leakage exclusion
**Never train on:** `actual_receipt_date`, `actual_lead_days`, `days_late`, `late_receipt_flag` (except as y target). Also do not use future delivery outcomes, future demand or realized event severity. Feature definitions and exact prediction timestamp must be frozen prior to model development.

## Reproduce and verify
From the extracted **v0.2 folder** (not your existing v0.1 notebook directory):

```bash
python verify_v02.py
```

To completely regenerate the v0.2 outputs from included synthetic seed tables:

```bash
python generate_v02.py
python verify_v02.py
```

Requires `pandas` and `numpy`. `generate_v02.py` uses fixed random seed `4202602`. Never overwrite the saved v0.1 baseline folder.
